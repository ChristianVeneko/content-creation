**The script** (`script.json`, 139 words): every line restates a fact above or in the first table. "He punched the ball into the net with his hand, in front of 114,580 people" (attendance; the fist); "the 51st minute of a World Cup quarter-final, Argentina against England"; "an England midfielder hooked the ball back toward his own goal"; "the keeper rushed out to meet it" ("Shilton moves out to meet the ball"); "Maradona leapt with his left arm up, and his fist got there first"; "floated over the keeper's head and dropped into the goal"; the protests and the referee; the quote, "After the game" = the post-match press conference; "Four minutes later" (51' and 55').

**On screen, kept generic:** the press-conference room, the TV room and the 1980s clock board are scenes we built (no claim about the real rooms or board); the clock shows only 51' and 55'. Shirt numbers: only Maradona's 10. Faces: generic MakeHuman bodies; no real face is modelled and no real person's face is shown close.

## Left out (couldn't verify, or sources disagree)
- Exact heights (18 cm vs 20 cm gap).
- Shirt numbers for anyone but Maradona's 10 (the brief allows only the 10).
- The referee as a figure on screen, the exact positions of the other players, the ball's make.
- Whether Hodge meant a pass or a clearance (FIFA says back-pass, others say clearance); the caption says "clearance loops back", which fits both.

## The look
- [lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar](https://github.com/lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar) (MIT code, CC BY 4.0 films), read 2026-11-02: the `styles/telestrator/STYLE.md` grammar (freeze after play, one freeze = one finding, flash + desaturate on the freeze, a red marker ring, a rewind with tracking bars). Nothing was installed or copied; our page is written from scratch.
- The 1986 TV look (4:3 picture, scanlines, grain, chroma bleed, a period score box) is our own shader.

## Media rules kept
No real footage, photos, broadcast commentary or crowd recordings. The sound is synthesized in numpy. Stylized figures with no faces: kit colours and the number 10 only. No FIFA, adidas, Umbro, Le Coq Sportif or broadcaster marks.
